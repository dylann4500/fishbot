\section{The instrument, and what it can see}
\label{sec:instrument}

Every claim in \S\ref{sec:results} and \S\ref{sec:exploit} is a claim about what an instrument
resolved. This section states the instrument's floor, its side-channel gate, and the arithmetic that
governs every interval in the report.

\subsection{Two floors, and they are different objects}
\label{sec:instrument-floor}

A \textbf{detection floor} is the effect size below which this instrument's fitted responders are
\emph{empty} rather than unresolved. Phase~1 measured it at \vsevenFloorPhaseOne{} points and
predicted it would buy down as (evaluation games)$^{-1/2}$. Phase~2 refuted that prediction at its own
predicted value: at exactly four times the games the floor is \vsevenPrimaryFloor{}, and the
sub-floor rung is still undetected --- not because the interval is wide but because the excess
resolves \emph{to} zero. \S\ref{sec:results-controls} reproduces that on holdout material.

The floor therefore does \textbf{not} buy down with games, and a 96,000-game cell does not lower it.
Two values are in force, and they are not interchangeable:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\vsevenPrimaryFloor{}} is the in-class detection floor. Applied to an
  \emph{exploitability} number it is a detection floor in its original sense. Applied to the primary
  claim of \S\ref{sec:results-frontier} --- a directly measured paired duplicate win-rate difference,
  with no responder, no fit and no maximisation in it --- it is a \emph{fixed pre-committed reference
  bar} and nothing more. The protocol does not claim a responder-recovery floor bounds that quantity,
  and neither does this report.
\item \textbf{\vsevenDeclFloor{}} is the declaration-family floor. It applies to exactly two things:
  the declaration-objective adversary of \S\ref{sec:exploit}, and the declaration-accuracy column of
  \S\ref{sec:results-frontier}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{How every interval in this report is computed}
\label{sec:instrument-arithmetic}

\begin{description}
\item[Edge.] A cell's win-rate advantage in points, $100 \times (\text{winRate}_A - 0.5)$. Every cell
  is a paired duplicate: the same deal bank, the same deal indices, and two rotations so each deal is
  played with the teams swapped, so the deal's intrinsic luck cancels.
\item[Interval.] The \textbf{deal-clustered bootstrap}, 20{,}000 resamples over deals. A Wilson
  interval is never quoted anywhere in this report: the two rotations of one deal are one correlated
  unit, and treating them as independent understates the width.
\item[Pooled.] Two banks of equal size: the mean of the two edges with
  $\text{se}_{\text{pooled}} = \sqrt{\text{se}_1^2 + \text{se}_2^2}/2$. Both per-bank values are
  printed beside every pooled figure in this report.
\item[Delta.] A difference between two \emph{pooled} cells: the difference of the estimates with the
  two pooled half-widths combined \textbf{in quadrature}. This is conservative --- the arms are paired
  on deals and the harness gives no paired delta across two separate cells --- and the protocol fixes
  it and says so.
\item[Replication.] A claim whose \textbf{sign} does not agree on both banks is reported as NOT
  REPLICATED whatever its pooled interval says. \S\ref{sec:against-location} is the case where that
  rule bites.
\end{description}

\subsection{The mechanical side-channel gate}
\label{sec:instrument-side}

The homogeneity constraint of \S\ref{sec:threat} had never been checked by anything but inspection.
\texttt{fish7 v7side} implements it: a listening-substitution test, a stream-independence test, a
posterior-invariance test, and a seat-isolation reconstruction test that replays every decision from
(own hand, public event stream, rules, reset seed) and counts what it cannot reproduce.

The gate is only worth its output if it is calibrated, so three positive controls were built, each a
policy carrying one planted cheat. Table~\ref{tab:sidechannel} is the holdout result:
each planted channel is caught by exactly the test built to catch it and by no other, identically on
both banks, over \vsevenSideCells{} cells.

\paragraph{The seat-isolation test measures a mode no scored cell uses, and that is stated rather
than buried.} Its zero-tolerance form requires one thread --- the audit depends on the deal-to-thread
partition, and work-stealing does not fix that partition at any higher count --- and agents rebuilt
per deal. Rebuilding agents per deal \emph{changes play}. What the gate certifies is therefore ``with
the cross-deal residue removed, nothing else depends on anything it should not''. The residue itself
is not removed from the shipped mode. \S\ref{sec:results-residual} measures it on holdout material and
reports the denominators moving between the two conditions, which is the same point restated as
data.
