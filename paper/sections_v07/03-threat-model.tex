\section{What ``exploitable'' was made to mean}
\label{sec:threat}

The project's standing reporting rule is that the worst case over the opponent panel leads and an
aggregate never does. That rule is correct and it had run out of discriminating power for the
comparison v0.7 has to make: a panel of hand-built archetypes measures robustness to the behaviours
someone thought to build, and every arm in this corpus beats most of them by
\vsevenFarSpanLo{} to \vsevenFarSpanHi{} points. A definition was therefore written before any v0.7 measurement was taken, and this section
states the parts of it that constrain how the numbers in \S\ref{sec:results} and \S\ref{sec:exploit}
may be read.

\paragraph{The unit is the team, not the seat.}
What is evaluated is the joint policy $\pi = (\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3)$ of three identical agents. The
headline adversary controls \emph{all three} opposing seats. A one-seat deviation column is reported
alongside it on the same deals, because a three-seat upgrade and a one-seat upgrade are different
quantities and the corpus has confused them before (\S\ref{sec:results-partners}).

\paragraph{The adversary is white-box, with an explicit exclusion list.}
It is given the target's source, its frozen parameter vector, and unlimited offline access to it. It
is \emph{not} given the deal, the deal seed, any seat's hand, or the engine's confidence field. The
exclusions are the point: a homogeneous team of three identical published deterministic agents
inverts the usual assumption of the team-correlation literature, where the correlation device is the
team's own and unknown to the opponent. Here the device is common knowledge, and the adversary class
built to exploit that --- transcript inversion against a deterministic team --- is the one
\S\ref{sec:exploit} reports as \texttt{Zeight}.

\paragraph{Homogeneity is two prohibitions, and it is checked mechanically.}
The team gets no shared secret pre-play seed. Everything else a seat-conditioned policy can do is
legal. An \emph{illegal} side channel is defined by the bridge encrypted-signal criterion, made
mechanical by a closed list of implementation-only discriminators plus four tests over all four engine
decision types. Until phase~3 none of those tests existed as code; the constraint had only ever been
checked by inspection. \S\ref{sec:instrument-side} reports the harness that implements them and the
three planted cheats that calibrate it.

\paragraph{Every exploitability number is a lower bound.}
Reported as $\ge x$ with class, correlation regime, seat count and budget attached. Computing the true
value is NP-hard even white-box. The consequence for this report is stated once and then honoured
everywhere: \textbf{a search that fails to find an exploit is evidence about the search, not a
property of the target.} The v0.4 record contains exactly this reading error, turning a null result
into a win, and \S\ref{sec:corrections} carries the correction forward rather than repeating it.

\paragraph{A class may contribute only after calibration.}
An adversary class is admitted to an exploitability claim only once it has passed a planted-edge
calibration at or below the effect size being claimed. That precondition is why
\S\ref{sec:instrument-floor} exists and why the negative controls of \S\ref{sec:results-controls}
are run at all: an instrument that cannot recover a planted edge of a given size cannot report the
absence of a real one of that size.

\paragraph{What the definition does not license.}
It does not license the claim that a policy passing every test is unexploitable, and it does not
license reading the panel's worst cell as an exploitability figure. The panel measures robustness
across playstyles; the adversary search measures what a fitted responder can do. They are different
objects and this report keeps them in different sections.
