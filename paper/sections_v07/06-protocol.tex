\section{The protocol, and the seal}
\label{sec:protocol}

The evaluation was run from a cleared context against a protocol committed before any holdout bank had
been played and before the sealed adversary half had been decoded. The phase read that protocol and
nothing else from the v0.7 document set: not the candidate report, not the adversary report, not any
training log. Where a training measurement is quoted for comparison it is quoted \emph{from the
protocol itself}, which states its expectations in advance for exactly that purpose --- so agreement
is a check and disagreement is a finding.

Three of the fourteen sealed adversaries are policies the rest of the panel already carries. They are
run anyway, because the protocol fixes the panel by name, and their agreement with the corresponding
scripted or frontier cell is used as a free determinism check
(\S\ref{sec:corrections-reproducibility}).

\subsection{The commit gate runs before any strength number}
\label{sec:protocol-gate}

A configuration that scores higher while failing the pathology rules is rejected, and the ordering is
not a formality: the corpus contains a configuration that scores 56.6\% against \vfour{} while
carrying 44.8\% dead asks and losing 14\% of its games to the action limit. Win rate and soundness are
dissociable in this family.

\subsection{What the protocol said would count as failure}
\label{sec:protocol-failure}

Seven conditions were written down in advance, ``because after the numbers are in every one of these
has a reading that makes it sound like something else''. They are reproduced in full in
Appendix~\ref{app:thresholds} and answered condition by condition in \S\ref{sec:against}. Two of them
came out against the release.

The protocol also fixed the primary bar. \fcheap{} is named rather than the deployed policy,
deliberately: the deployed policy ships its search off, beating it is the easier claim, and an
earlier phase had already shown that several unfitted one-switch deviations of the incumbent do it.
The v0.7 case had to be against the frontier.

And it fixed, in advance, the sentence to be used if the partner-regime ordering survived --- naming
the direction so it could not be chosen afterwards, and recording that on training material that
comparison goes \emph{against} the release. \S\ref{sec:results-partners} reports what happened and
uses the sentence.

\subsection{Deviations are recorded next to the result they affect}
\label{sec:protocol-deviations}

The protocol requires a deviation for anything the evaluation did that it does not specify: a cell
dropped, a cell added, a threshold read a particular way, a command whose flags differ. There are
\vsevenDeviations{} of them, \vsevenDeviationAdded{} adding cells or checks the protocol's own
questions turn out not to be computable without, and \vsevenDeviationCorrections{} of them
corrections. Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} is the full register, read out of the results document
rather than retyped. \vsevenCellsDropped{} preregistered cells were dropped.
