\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This paper is the account of record for a research programme in six-player Canadian Fish, a team
card game with a property that is rare and useful: \emph{every action is public}. Cards move only
through asks whose target and outcome everyone observes, so the entire hidden state is the initial
deal, and the posterior over it is exactly computable in closed form. A domain in which inference
can be made provably correct is a domain in which every remaining loss is attributable to policy ---
and that separation is what the programme has spent six versions exploiting.

\S\ref{sec:game} states the game completely, so that nothing here depends on outside material, and
\S\ref{sec:domain} says why it is worth studying. The short version is three properties in
combination. The hidden state is a single object under hard logical constraints, so exact inference
is cheap rather than aspirational. The unit of play is a three-player team whose members cannot
communicate, which is the decentralised control problem in its purest card-game form. And a team of
three \emph{identical, deterministic, published} agents inverts the standard assumption of the
team-correlation literature, in which the correlation device is private: here it is common
knowledge, and an adversary may read it.

\paragraph{What this paper contains.}
The programme has produced two kinds of result, and this paper treats them as equally important.

The first kind is about the game. An exactly computable observer-conditioned deal posterior
(\S\ref{sec:engine-belief}) --- and the finding that substituting it for the deployed approximation
makes the policy \emph{worse}, because exactness in this game is purchased by discarding behaviour
(\S\ref{sec:arc-vsix-belief}). More than half of ask decisions ending in a bit-for-bit tie that the
\emph{exact} posterior also cannot separate, making the largest item on the design register a
property of the information set rather than a defect (\S\ref{sec:arc-vsix-ties}). A determinized
search that is \vsixCurseGap{}~points \emph{worse} than the blueprint it searches from when its
action is chosen by an unguarded argmax, and fully recovered by a paired lower-confidence-bound
guard (\S\ref{sec:arc-vsix-search}).

The second kind is about measurement, and it is roughly half the contribution.
\S\ref{sec:methodology} presents the apparatus as method rather than housekeeping: the
duplicate-block design, deal-clustered intervals, power computed before a cell is run, replication
across disjoint banks used as a veto over pooled significance, sealed material with committed
digests, preregistration with stated failure conditions, a commit gate that runs before any strength
number, calibrated detection floors, and a mechanical homogeneity gate with planted positive
controls. Every one of those components was built in response to a specific measured failure, and
most of the failures were this project's own (\S\ref{sec:arc-vsix-apparatus}).

\paragraph{The final cycle, and its verdict.}
\S\S\ref{sec:instrument}--\ref{sec:results} report a cycle designed so that its answer could not be
argued into being positive: characterise the instrument, search for adversaries in the open, build
candidate architectures, freeze exactly one configuration, and evaluate it from a cleared context
against a protocol committed before any holdout deal had been played.

The preregistered primary claim passes and is certified: \vsevenBtwoFcheap{}~points
$[\vsevenBtwoFcheapLo, \vsevenBtwoFcheapHi]$ against the cheapest point of the previous frontier,
over \vsevenBtwoFcheapN{} games of sealed material, replicating on both banks and gate-clean. Nothing
built to exploit it succeeds; it is not a self-play convention.

\textbf{And it does not beat the composite the programme had already assembled two phases earlier}
--- \vsevenBtwoComposite{}~points $[\vsevenBtwoCompositeLo, \vsevenBtwoCompositeHi]$. The protocol
stated in advance what that would mean, and \S\ref{sec:against} says it: the strength gain in this
cycle is real, it was found by an unfitted coordinate sweep, and the architecture work that followed
added no measurable strength on top of it. Two further results point the same way. On a
\vsevenPanelMembers{}-member panel the frozen configuration is \emph{third of four} on minimax
regret. And the one quantity that falls short of its advance-stated training value is the group of
keys the freeze was \emph{selected over}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A complete, self-contained treatment of a domain with no prior computational
  literature} (\S\S\ref{sec:game}--\ref{sec:engine}): rules, dialect, the exact posterior and its
  closed form, and the verification suite that establishes the engine is what it claims to be.

\item \textbf{An evaluation apparatus presented as adoptable method} (\S\ref{sec:methodology}), each
  component paired with the failure it prevents --- including two occasions on which this project's
  own published claims turned out to be artefacts of its instrument.

\item \textbf{An operational definition of team exploitability} for a homogeneous team of published
  deterministic agents (\S\ref{sec:threat}), made mechanical by four tests with three calibrated
  positive controls.

\item \textbf{Four candidate architectures killed with measurements} (\S\ref{sec:rejected}): a
  declaration channel that closes at exactly zero, a leaf evaluator fitted \vsevenKoneFold$\times$
  better that buys nothing, per-decision objectives that produce worse policies while moving their
  own proxies the right way, and a learned re-ranker worth \vsevenKfiveHeadToHead{}~$\pm$
  \vsevenKfivePm{} against a free hash tie-break.

\item \textbf{A preregistered evaluation with a stated failure condition} (\S\ref{sec:protocol}), of
  which two conditions came out against the release and are reported as such
  (\S\ref{sec:against}).

\item \textbf{A corrections register} (\S\ref{sec:corrections}) against both the corpus's record and
  this cycle's own, and \textbf{an explicit statement of what a near-optimality claim would require}
  (\S\ref{sec:towardone}), none of which has been produced.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.}
It does not claim the release is stronger than the best configuration available before the
architecture work began; the cell that would show that contains zero. It does not claim the frozen
configuration is hard to exploit --- only that eight searches, at a stated budget, in stated
classes, did not exploit it, which is a lower bound within a searched class and nothing more
(\S\ref{sec:limitations-lbr}). It does not claim the gain is located: the protocol's own location
test does not replicate across banks (\S\ref{sec:against-location}). And it does not claim that any
policy here is near-optimal, or that this is the strongest Fish-playing team, because the comparison
class has one member and every exploitability number in this corpus is a lower bound from a search
rather than a bound on what a search could find.
