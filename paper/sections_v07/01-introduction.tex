\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Six-player Canadian Fish is a team game of pure public information flow. Fifty-four cards are dealt
nine each into two alternating teams of three; a player asks a named opponent for a named card, must
already hold another card of that card's six-card half-suit, must not hold the card itself, and keeps
the turn on a hit and loses it on a miss; a team scores a half-suit by naming, correctly and in full,
which of its members holds each of the six cards. Every ask, every hit, every miss and every
declaration is seen by all six players, which makes the entire hidden state the initial deal and the
posterior over that deal exactly computable.

The \vsix{} study ended with a policy that beat its predecessor by about nine tenths of a point and
with a design register most of whose entries it had just closed as worth nothing. It named the
binding constraint plainly: at the budget the project had always used, this policy class looks flat.
The v0.7 programme was built to find out whether it \emph{is} flat, and it was organised so that the
answer could not be argued into being positive. Five phases ran under separate briefs: rebuild the
measuring instrument and characterise what it can see; generate adversaries in the open and find what
actually beats the \vsix{} frontier; build candidate architectures; run a bake-off and freeze exactly
one configuration; and then --- from a cleared context, reading only a protocol committed before any
holdout deal had been played --- measure the frozen configuration on sealed material.

This report is phase 6. It is written after the numbers are in, and it is bound by what the protocol
said before they were.

\paragraph{The result in one paragraph.}
The preregistered primary claim passes and is certified: against \fcheap{}, the cheapest point of the
\vsix{} frontier, the frozen configuration scores \vsevenBtwoFcheap{}~points
$[\vsevenBtwoFcheapLo, \vsevenBtwoFcheapHi]$ over \vsevenBtwoFcheapN{} games of sealed material, with
the sign replicating on both banks and a lower bound above the pre-committed detection floor. Nothing
built to exploit it succeeds. It is not a self-play convention. But it does \emph{not} beat
\composite{} --- \vsevenBtwoComposite{}~points
$[\vsevenBtwoCompositeLo, \vsevenBtwoCompositeHi]$ --- and the protocol states in advance what that
means: the increment that phases~3 and~4 added to what phase~2 already had did not replicate, and on
that cell the release reduces to a phase-2 result. The strength gain in this cycle is real, it is
located in a single coordinate found by an unfitted sweep in phase~2, and the three phases of
architecture work that followed added no measurable strength on top of it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A preregistered evaluation with a stated failure condition}
  (\S\ref{sec:protocol}). The protocol names the bar (\fcheap{}, not the deployed policy), the
  thresholds, the seven conditions that would mean v0.7 is not an advancement, and the sentence the
  report must use if the partner-regime ordering survives. Two of the seven conditions came out
  against the release and are reported as such (\S\ref{sec:against}).

\item \textbf{The worst case and the minimax regret lead, and they disagree}
  (\S\ref{sec:results-panel}). On a \vsevenPanelMembers{}-member panel scored identically for four
  arms, \frozen{}'s worst cell is \vsevenWorstFrozen{} against \incumbent{}'s
  \vsevenWorstIncumbent{} --- but \composite{}'s is better still on both readings
  (\vsevenWorstComposite{} with its free self-cell, \vsevenWorstCompositeExSelf{} without), and
  \frozen{} is \emph{third of four} on minimax regret. Both are reported; the panel mean is reported last and
  labelled a diagnostic, because the project's standing rule forbids leading with an aggregate.

\item \textbf{Four architectures killed with measurements} (\S\ref{sec:rejected}). The
  declaration-allocation channel --- the corpus's second-largest quantified opportunity --- closes at
  exactly zero, and its proposed fix is shown not to be a fix. A leaf evaluator fitted
  \vsevenKoneFold$\times$ better in the only statistic its consumer can use buys nothing in play.
  Every self-oriented per-decision objective produces a worse policy while moving its own proxy in
  the intended direction. A learned re-ranker inside the exchangeable tie group is worth
  \vsevenKfiveHeadToHead{} $\pm$ \vsevenKfivePm{} against a free hash tie-break.

\item \textbf{A mechanical side-channel gate with calibrated positive controls}
  (\S\ref{sec:instrument-side}). Three planted cheats each fail exactly the tests the
  protocol requires of them --- two for one of the three, not one each --- and pass every other test,
  on holdout material, across \vsevenSideCells{} cells.

\item \textbf{A fresh adversary search that finds nothing} (\S\ref{sec:exploit}). Eight independent
  searches over four objectives and three classes, each evaluated on banks disjoint from the stream it
  was fitted on. Every arm loses; the largest upper bound anywhere is \vsevenBfourWorstUpper{}.

\item \textbf{The selection signature, located} (\S\ref{sec:against-selection}). Of four quantities
  with a training figure stated in advance, \vsevenSelReproduce{} reproduce or exceed it and the
  largest shortfall falls on the one measuring the group of keys the freeze was selected over.

\item \textbf{Corrections to the record} (\S\ref{sec:corrections}), including a freeze-verification
  step that could not fire because its own baseline had been refreshed, a scored-cell count that the
  artifacts do not support, and a cost figure the corpus understated --- by
  \vsevenCostFcheapTight$\times$ against \vsevenCostRecorded$\times$ for the configuration the
  record was describing.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.}
It does not claim that v0.7 is stronger than the best configuration available before phase~3 began;
the cell that would show that is \vsevenBtwoComposite{}~points with an interval containing zero. It
does not claim that the frozen configuration is hard to exploit --- only that eight searches at a
stated budget, in stated classes, did not exploit it, which is a lower bound within a searched class
and nothing more (\S\ref{sec:limitations-lbr}). It does not claim the gain is located: the protocol's
own location test does not replicate across the two banks (\S\ref{sec:against-location}). And it does
not claim that any policy in this corpus is near-optimal. \S\ref{sec:towardone} states what would
have to be measured before that sentence could be written about \emph{any} Fish-playing team, and
none of it has been.
