\section{The engine, and exact inference over deals}
\label{sec:engine}

Everything in this paper is measured by one program, and half the paper's contribution is the
discipline that program enforces. This section describes it: the simulator, the information barrier
between the rules and the policies, the exact deal posterior, and the verification that all three
are what they claim to be.

\subsection{The simulator}
\label{sec:engine-impl}

The engine is a single-header C++20 core. A hand is one 64-bit word over the 54 card indices
$c = 6h + i$ for half-suit $h$ and offset $i$, so hand arithmetic is bit arithmetic. Random streams
are counter-based and keyed by \emph{(seed, seat, purpose)}, which is the property everything else
rests on: \textbf{a deal and its entire play reproduce from the seed alone}. The duplicate-block
protocol of \S\ref{sec:methodology-duplicate} depends on that and on nothing else.

Throughput on the deployed policy is \vsixThroughputSix{} complete six-player games per second,
against \vsixThroughputFive{} for its predecessor, with exact inference running in every seat.
Every compute figure in this paper is single-machine, measured under load, and therefore a lower
bound.

\subsection{Information safety}
\label{sec:engine-safety}

The acting policy never receives the game state. It receives its own \texttt{Knowledge} object ---
the constraint bookkeeping of \S\ref{sec:engine-belief} --- plus its own hand and the public event
stream. There is no pointer from a policy to the deal, to another seat's hand, or to the referee.
This is an architectural barrier rather than a convention, and it is what makes the side-channel
audit of \S\ref{sec:methodology-side} a test of the \emph{policy} rather than of the harness: a
policy that cannot reach the deal cannot cheat by reading it, so any decision the audit fails to
reproduce from public information indicates state the policy carried across deals, not information
it stole from the referee.

\subsection{The hidden state is the initial deal}
\label{sec:engine-belief}

Because every card movement is public (\S\ref{sec:domain-exact}), an observer's uncertainty is
entirely about the initial deal, and the observer's information is a conjunction of hard
constraints on it:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[(C1)] its own nine cards, known exactly;
\item[(C2)] each other seat's current card count, known exactly;
\item[(C3)] every card that has been transferred, and to whom;
\item[(C4)] for each failed ask, that the target did not hold the named card at that moment;
\item[(C5)] for each ask, that the asker held at least one other card of the named half-suit and did
      not hold the named card.
\end{enumerate}

The counting problem this induces would in general be a permanent-like enumeration over the whole
deal, which is the object the literature approximates with Ryser's formula \cite{ryser}, Glynn's
\cite{glynn}, the Jerrum--Sinclair--Vigoda chain \cite{jsv}, matrix scaling \cite{lsw} or sequential
importance sampling \cite{chen-sis}.

\textbf{Fish sidesteps all of it, because the constraints factor by half-suit.} Constraint (C5) is
about a half-suit; (C4) is about a card, hence about a half-suit; the only cross-half-suit coupling
is (C2), the per-seat card counts. So the count decomposes into a per-half-suit block enumeration
joined by a small dynamic program over the count vector. The result is exact in closed form:
$Z$, the per-card marginals, the probability a team owns a half-suit, and the probability of a named
allocation, all without sampling. An exact rebuild costs \vsixCountCost{}~ms, which is why this
project has been able to treat inference as solved and spend its cycles on policy.

\subsection{The policy prior, and the object the policy actually deploys}
\label{sec:engine-prior}

The exact posterior is exact \emph{under a uniform prior over deals consistent with the
constraints}. It contains no model of behaviour: the block construction consumes the deduction state
alone and has no parameter through which an opponent's tendencies could enter.

The deployed path adds one: a fitted soft reweighting of the rules posterior with two parameters and
no learned opponent model, in the spirit of the Skat inference line
\cite{solinas-supervised,rebstock-inference,buro-kermit} but far simpler. The shipped weight is
$\theta = \vsixPriorTheta{}$.

This is where the corpus's most-cited internal result lives, and \S\ref{sec:arc-vsix-belief} states
it: scored as predictors on identical states, the \emph{exact} posterior is the worst of the three
inference paths measured. It is the reason this project's standing methodological rule is that
``exact Bayesian inference'' is an assumption to test rather than a property to assume --- a rule
that paid off a second time in \S\ref{sec:rejected-alloc}.

\subsection{Verification}
\label{sec:engine-verify}

Three independent checks run over the engine, and all three ran again as preconditions of the
evaluation in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\begin{description}
\item[Soundness audit.] Every seat's deduced knowledge is checked against the true deal at every
  decision: no agent's deduction may ever exclude the truth. The evaluation reported here recorded
  \vsevenVerifyViolations{} violations in \vsevenVerifyChecks{} checks, and the preceding cycle
  \vsixAuditViolations{} in \vsixAuditChecks{}.
\item[Cross-engine agreement.] The block dynamic program, an independent card-counting path and an
  exact sampler are required to agree. The self-test reports \vsevenSelftestChecks{} checks passing,
  with block-versus-card-DP agreement at the limit of double precision.
\item[Identity control.] The current policy, carrying its predecessor's parameter vector with every
  new mechanism switched off, must produce a transcript identical to its predecessor's under md5.
  It does (\vsixIdentity{}). This is what makes an ablation an ablation: without it, a measured
  difference could be an accident of refactoring.
\end{description}

Two engine defects found by these checks are worth naming here, because both changed previously
published numbers and both are the kind of defect that survives precisely because it is invisible to
the deployed path.

\paragraph{The exact reference engine was unsound in multi-agent play.} The block dynamic program
parked every instance's tables in a shared per-thread pool, so any second construction on the same
thread silently repointed the first instance's tables: \numAliasMismatch{} mismatches in
\numAliasChecks{} checks. Every previously published number computed with the exact belief in a
configuration where more than one seat holds such an object is affected. The deployed
approximate-belief numbers are not --- which is exactly why the defect survived three studies. It is
repaired at the query level; raw reads of the shared pool still differ (\vsixAliasRawMismatch{}) and
are documented rather than fixed, because the pool is what keeps the memory footprint of six
concurrent observers bounded.

\paragraph{A declaration pre-gate audit was dead code.} The audit that verifies the declaration
pre-gate never rejects a \emph{correct} declaration was parsed only in an older branch and so had
never run. Once enabled it reports \vsixGateFalseNeg{} false negatives over
\vsixGateOpportunities{} declaration opportunities and \vsixGateRejected{} gate rejections: the
pre-gates are exact.
