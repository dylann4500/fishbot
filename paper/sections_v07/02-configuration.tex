\section{What v0.7 changes}
\label{sec:configuration}

v0.7 is \textbf{a configuration of the \vsix{} policy family, not a new architecture}. That sentence
is the protocol's, written before the evaluation ran, and the evaluation did not disturb it. The
frozen object is \vsix{}'s fitted \vsevenVsixCoords{}-coordinate vector, one hand-set extended coordinate, three
switches that turn off machinery an earlier phase measured as defective, one new mechanism, and the
truncated test-time search that \vsix{} shipped switched off:

\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily\raggedright
\vsevenFrozenSpecWrapped{}
\end{quote}

\noindent
It is written to \filepath{engine/fishbot\_v07.json} by \filepath{engine/freeze\_config\_v07.py},
which \emph{executes} its round-trip assertion rather than printing it: the evaluation reconstructed
the spec from that file and never typed it. The artifact carries
\vsevenCoordinates{} resolved coordinates and the mirror pathology digest
\texttt{\vsevenMirrorDigest{}}.

\subsection{The five keys, and what each was expected to be worth}
\label{sec:configuration-keys}

\begin{description}

\item[\texttt{r12=25} --- half-suit contestation.] The information-denial coordinate
  \texttt{oppCertDonate}, found by an \emph{unfitted} coordinate sweep in phase~2 rather than by any
  architecture work. Phase~2 measured a single hand-set setting of it at \vsevenOneSwitchArm{}~points
  against the deployed policy over \vsevenPhaseTwoCell{} games with no fit at all. The protocol states in advance
  that this key carries the gain and that a holdout leave-one-out drop of roughly two points is the
  check: \vsevenTrainRtwelveLoo{} on training material. \S\ref{sec:results-attribution} reports what
  holdout gives.

\item[The endgame-truncated search.] \texttt{s1=1,det=12,cand=4,kappa=2.5,rbelief=indep,depth=12,maxq=26}
  --- machinery \vsix{} built, measured and shipped \emph{off}. It is a determinized search that
  reconstructs all six continuation players at their own information sets rather than making them
  clairvoyant, with a paired lower-confidence-bound deviation rule. Its leave-one-out value on
  training material is \vsevenTrainSearchLoo{}.

\item[\texttt{rtie=1} --- decorrelating the tie group.] \vsevenTieContested\% of contested ask
  decisions in \vsix{} tie bit-for-bit and are decided by an unstable \texttt{std::sort} order. This
  key replaces that order with a hash of the public event stream. Training leave-one-out
  \vsevenTrainRtieLoo{}.

\item[\texttt{pool=-1,oppfloor=-1,force=1000000,askfloor=-1} --- the urgency escalation off.] These
  switch off \vfive{}'s 220-event declaration clock. Behind that clock sits a
  \vsevenCliff-point cliff: past the threshold the declaration rule drops its team-ownership floor and
  cashes half-suits at close to a coin flip. No adversary ever reached it --- the longest game any
  phase-2 adversary produced against an unmodified incumbent is \vsevenCliffIncumbentTail{} events ---
  but a candidate that simply switches the clock off inherits its termination duty. Training
  leave-one-out \vsevenTrainUrgoffLoo{}.

\item[\texttt{stall=12} --- the replacement termination guarantee.] Escalate after twelve consecutive
  public events in which this seat's hard-certificate hash does not change. The threshold is established at zero game cost: over \vsevenStallMirrorGames{} mirror games \vsix{} never produces a no-progress run longer than
  \vsevenStallIncumbentRun{}, and a \emph{deadlocked} configuration --- the gate's negative control,
  not \frozen{} --- produces \vsevenStallFrozenRun{}. Any threshold in $[12, 60]$ is simultaneously
  unreachable in ordinary play and immediate in a deadlock; the separation between the two regimes is
  fifty-fold, though the threshold's own margin over observed ordinary play is twelve against six. Armed, its mirror digest is byte-identical to the same configuration
  without it: the rule's cost in ordinary play is not small, it is identically zero, because it never
  runs.

\end{description}

\noindent
The last three are individually indistinguishable from zero at the margin. \emph{As a group} they were
measured at \vsevenTrainGroup{} $[\vsevenTrainGroupLo, \vsevenTrainGroupHi]$ on training material,
replicated on both training banks, as a paired head-to-head against \composite{}. The protocol
forbids attributing that group's value to any one of them, and \S\ref{sec:results-attribution} keeps
that discipline. One further key, \texttt{m2=0}, was dropped from the freeze because its
leave-one-out drop is exactly zero; it is measured anyway, because the ablation lattice preregisters
the cell.

\subsection{What was deliberately not changed}
\label{sec:configuration-notchanged}

No fit of any kind was applied to the frozen configuration. Its vector is \vsix{}'s, found by
\vsix{}'s repaired optimiser under a minimax-regret objective over a four-member panel. The
architecture work of phase~3 produced five candidates and contributed one of them --- the defect
stack above --- and contributed it as a configuration change plus one small mechanism rather than as
an architecture. \S\ref{sec:rejected} is what happened to the other four, and it is the more valuable
half of the cycle.

The engine defect that the evaluation runs \emph{under} is also deliberate. Agents are constructed
once per thread and reused across every deal that thread is handed, and some per-agent state survives
\texttt{reset()} and is reachable only under the search. Phase~4 named the carrier ---
\texttt{\vsevenSsixCarrier{}}, the rollout engine, which \texttt{reset()} never touches --- and did
not fix it, because the one-line repair changes the play of every searching configuration by about one
deal in \vsevenSsixIncidence{}, and shipping that after the freeze is the one thing a freeze exists to
prevent. It is a named, quantified engine defect, every strength number in this report is measured
under it, and \S\ref{sec:results-residual} measures it again on holdout material.
