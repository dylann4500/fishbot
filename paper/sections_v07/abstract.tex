\vsix{} left a policy class that three studies had failed to improve. This report tests one
configuration of that class against material sealed before the configuration existed, under a
protocol committed in advance that states what result would count as failure. The battery is
\vsevenScoredCells{} scored cells, \vsevenScoredGames{} games, \vsevenAuditViolations{} audit
violations, every preregistered cell measured and none dropped.

The preregistered primary claim passes. Against \fcheap{} --- the cheapest point of the \vsix{}
frontier, named as the bar rather than the deployed policy --- \frozen{} scores
\textbf{\vsevenBtwoFcheap{} points} $[\vsevenBtwoFcheapLo, \vsevenBtwoFcheapHi]$ over
\vsevenBtwoFcheapN{} games with the sign replicating on both banks, a lower bound above the
pre-committed \vsevenPrimaryFloor{}-point detection floor, and a clean commit gate:
\textbf{\vsevenPrimaryVerdict{}}. On the \vsevenPanelMembers{}-member shared panel its worst cell is
\vsevenWorstFrozen{} $[\vsevenWorstFrozenLo, \vsevenWorstFrozenHi]$ against the incumbent's
\vsevenWorstIncumbent{}, and it is the only arm that beats the hardest sealed adversary. Eight
independent fresh adversary searches, the white-box inverter included, all lose; the largest upper
bound anywhere is \vsevenBfourWorstUpper{}, which is \vsevenBfourFloorGap{} points below the floor.
The advantage is not a self-play convention: it survives partner substitution with a minimum of
\vsevenSoneMin{} across \vsevenSoneRows{} changed-partner settings, and cross-play between three
independently-trained runs moves it by \vsevenXpGapAbs{} points.

\textbf{And it is not an advance on what was already in hand.} \frozen{} does not beat
\composite{} --- \vsevenBtwoComposite{} points $[\vsevenBtwoCompositeLo, \vsevenBtwoCompositeHi]$, an
interval containing zero --- so the increment an earlier phase reported did not replicate, and on
that cell the release reduces to a configuration phase~2 of this programme already had. The
protocol named that outcome in advance and named its consequence: the contribution is then the
measurement programme and the closed directions, not the policy. Two further results point the same
way. \frozen{} is \emph{third of four} on minimax regret over the shared panel
(\vsevenRegretFrozen{} against \vsevenRegretBest{}'s \vsevenRegretFcheap{}), so the arm with the
best worst case is not the arm with the best regret. And the one quantity that falls short of its
training value is the group of keys the freeze was \emph{selected over}, by
\vsevenSelWorstShortfall{} points against an expected maximum under the null of
\vsevenSelExpectedMax{} --- the selection signature appearing exactly where selection happened and
nowhere else.

The value of the cycle is therefore the negative half. Five candidate architectures were built and
four died with measurements rather than arguments: the declaration-allocation channel, the corpus's
second-largest quantified opportunity, closes at exactly zero, its named mechanism shown not to
exist over $\sim$\vsevenLoneDecls{} recorded declarations; a leaf evaluator fitted
\vsevenKoneFold$\times$ better buys nothing, because the deviation rule it feeds is nearly
leaf-invariant; every self-oriented per-decision objective produces a \emph{worse} policy while
moving its own proxy in the intended direction; and a learned re-ranker inside the exchangeable tie
group is worth \vsevenKfiveHeadToHead{} $\pm$ \vsevenKfivePm{} against a free hash tie-break. What
remains is a policy class in which the only located gain is a single coordinate found by an
unfitted sweep, and an evaluation that costs \vsevenCostFrozenTight$\times$ the blueprint --- not
the \vsevenCostRecorded$\times$ the record carried --- to resolve it. This report states the barrier,
lists what would have to be measured before any Fish-playing team could credibly be called
near-optimal, and records \vsevenDeviations{} deviations and
\vsevenDeviationCorrections{} corrections against its own protocol and the corpus's record.
