Six-player Canadian Fish is a team card game in which every action is public, so the entire hidden
state is the initial deal and the posterior over it is exactly computable. That combination ---
tractable exact inference, three-player teams that cannot communicate, and a deterministic published
policy whose coordination is therefore common knowledge --- makes it an unusually clean laboratory
for separating what a policy knows from what it does. We know of no prior published computational
work on the six-player game.

This paper is the complete account of a six-version programme in that domain. It reports an exactly
computable observer-conditioned deal posterior, and the finding that substituting it for the
deployed approximation makes the policy \emph{worse}, because exactness here is bought by discarding
behaviour; a determinized search that is \vsixCurseGap{}~points worse than its own blueprint under an
unguarded argmax, and recovered entirely by a paired lower-confidence-bound guard; and a measurement
crisis in which five mechanisms cleared a 95\% paired interval at one budget and returned exactly
zero at three times it. Roughly half the contribution is the apparatus built in response: a
duplicate-block design, deal-clustered intervals, power computed before a cell is run, replication
across disjoint banks as a veto over pooled significance, sealed holdout material with committed
digests, preregistration with stated failure conditions, a commit gate that runs before any strength
number, calibrated detection floors, and a mechanical homogeneity gate with planted positive
controls.

The programme's final cycle put one frozen configuration through that apparatus on material sealed
before the configuration existed. The preregistered primary claim passes and is certified: against
the cheapest point of the previous frontier the configuration scores \vsevenBtwoFcheap{}~points
$[\vsevenBtwoFcheapLo, \vsevenBtwoFcheapHi]$ over \vsevenBtwoFcheapN{} games, replicating on both
banks. Eight independent adversary searches, including a white-box inverter that reads the target's
own deterministic policy, all lose. The advantage is not a self-play convention: a single seat's
upgrade is worth \vsevenOneSeatFive{} of the \vsevenSoneSelf{}-point three-seat gain, and three
independently-trained runs cross-play within \vsevenXpGapAbs{}~points of their own diagonal.

\textbf{And it is not an advance on what the programme already had.} The configuration does not beat
the composite assembled two phases earlier --- \vsevenBtwoComposite{}~points
$[\vsevenBtwoCompositeLo, \vsevenBtwoCompositeHi]$ --- so the increment the intervening architecture
work contributed did not replicate. On a \vsevenPanelMembers{}-member panel scored identically for
four arms it is \emph{third of four} on minimax regret, and its own headline worst cell does not
replicate in sign. The one quantity falling short of its advance-stated training value is the group
of keys the freeze was \emph{selected over}, by \vsevenSelWorstShortfall{}~points against an expected
maximum under the null of \vsevenSelExpectedMax{}.

The residue is therefore negative and, we argue, more durable than the release: four candidate
architectures killed with measurements rather than arguments, including a declaration channel that
closes at exactly zero and a leaf evaluator fitted \vsevenKoneFold$\times$ better that buys nothing;
a corrections register against this project's own record; and a named barrier --- this policy class
converts mechanism into win rate at a rate below a detection floor that does not fall as evaluation
games increase. We state what evidence would be required before any Fish-playing team could
credibly be called near-optimal, and none of it has been produced.
