\section{Threats to validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

Limitations are grouped by the kind of validity each threatens; caveats stated
in full at the point of measurement are cross-referenced rather than restated.

\textbf{Internal validity.} Every ablation is a frozen-policy ablation with the
\numParams{} fitted coordinates left as selected, so no row measures a
component's standalone value (\S\ref{sec:ablations}). The \numAskFeats{} ask
features are correlated and the paired design controls the deal, not the
collinearity, so a component can look essential because the surrounding weights
lean on it, or redundant because a correlated term covers it.
Substituting the reference belief compounds this, changing the inference, every
feature computed from it and the constants they are compared against
(\S\ref{sec:discussion}). No retrained ablation was run for any row.

\textbf{Construct validity.} The posterior is exact with respect to the rules
and the uniform prior over consistent deals, not to opponents' policies;
\eqref{eq:prior} is a parametric stand-in for that correction, not the correction
(\S\ref{sec:policyprior}). The declaration rule \eqref{eq:stop} compares fitted
linear value estimates under an approximate team-level feature transition rather
than true continuation values, and is not a Bellman-optimal stopping value
(\S\ref{sec:declare}). The two declaration
pre-gates are pruning heuristics with a measured, non-zero false-negative rate
(\S\ref{sec:results-verify}). And win rate, the primary metric, does not capture
the half-suit differential, declaration reliability or calibration reported
beside it (\S\ref{sec:protocol-metrics}).

\textbf{External validity.} Deals were held out from fitting and selection;
opponent identities largely were not (\S\ref{sec:results-h2h},
\S\ref{sec:protocol-seeds}). Every opponent was written by us or ported from
v0.3, and with no expert human logs for this dialect and no independently
authored Fish engine to play, nothing tests the agent against a strategy source
outside this project.

\textbf{Statistical validity.} Win-rate intervals are percentile bootstrap
intervals over \numBootstrapDraws{} resamples clustered on the deal
(\S\ref{sec:protocol-ci}); such intervals under-cover in evaluations of this
kind \cite{jordan-rleval}, and neither BCa nor bootstrap-$t$ was implemented.
The calibration metrics carry no clustered uncertainty at all
(\S\ref{sec:results-calib}). Many rows of Table~\ref{tab:ablations} have
intervals including zero: the study was not powered below about a point, and an
unresolved row is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

\textbf{Opponent-population dependence.} Leading every member of this panel is
not being unexploitable, and nothing here bounds exploitability from above: the
response probe searches one parametric class with one optimiser at a fixed
budget, so its score is a lower bound within that class (\S\ref{sec:lbr}), and a
differently shaped adversary or a wider search could do better.

\textbf{Rules-dialect dependence.} All results are for the dialect of
\S\ref{sec:rules}, given in full in \S\ref{app:dialect}; \S\ref{app:dialects}
varies three rules and the three arbitration orders, not every consequential
choice, and arbitration by lowest seat is itself a modelling choice, not a rule.
The asks inside a locked half-suit that only the owning team may make are a
channel the fitted configuration has no term for (\S\ref{sec:locked}):
unexploited here, not shown to be worthless.

\textbf{Computational and model-class limits.} The policy class is small --- a
\numAskFeats-feature linear ask score, \numKnobs{} decision knobs and a
\numValueFeats-feature linear value function --- which caps what the one-ply
lookahead and the declaration rule built on it can extract. No goodness-of-fit
figure is reported for the deployed value coefficients: the in-sample
$R^2 = \numValueRSq$ of E14 belongs to the artifact's ridge fit, whose
coefficients are not the compiled ones, and neither vector was evaluated out of
sample (\S\ref{app:params}). Termination is imposed by hand-set escalation
horizons rather than derived, and a differently tuned pair of policies could
cycle inside them (\S\ref{sec:termination}). The brute-force oracle validates
only small reachable states, \numOracleStatesSkipped{} having been skipped as
too large (\S\ref{sec:results-verify}). And the base seed of fitting round 5,
which produced the shipping configuration, is in no committed script or artifact,
so that round cannot be reproduced from scratch (\S\ref{sec:fitting}).

\textbf{Experiments that would resolve the open questions}, each targeting one
limitation above, none of which was run:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0pt,topsep=2pt,parsep=0pt]
\item Matched-budget fits against the Fast path and against the reference engine
on a common held-out bank, in place of the non-invariance result.
\item Retrained ablations for the C5 certificates, the policy prior and the
belief mode.
\item A declaration-timing experiment sized for effects below a point.
\item Deal-clustered intervals for the calibration metrics.
\item An opponent family excluded from all fitting and selection, ideally an
independently authored engine or expert human logs.
\item A held-out corpus and a goodness-of-fit figure for the deployed value
coefficients.
\item For termination, a constructive reachable recurrent state whose
information state also repeats.
\end{itemize}
