TERMINATION ARGUMENT, CORRECTED.  The header comment inherited from the earlier
attempt claimed a total termination proof.  It is partial.  The progress hash
includes handCount[], so a SUCCESSFUL ask always counts as progress even though
a card moving between two seats is not a monotone gain; the monotone argument
(mask bits, resolved cards, declared half-suits) bounds only the
no-new-certificate mode of non-termination.  That is the mode that actually
occurs — v0.4's dead-ask loop, which is what the urgency-off configurations
regress into — and the bound that carries is the measured distribution above.
The comment in v07_stall.hpp was rewritten to say this.

7. THE STOCHASTIC LIMB — priced, and the price is zero
=============================================================================
THREAT-MODEL H1 permits independent per-seat randomisation (only a SHARED secret
is forbidden), but the hierarchy v_Com >= v_Cor >= v_No predicts it should cost
something, and the threat model says in terms that this is "the trade phase 3
must price, not assume".  `rtie=1` is not that trade: it is seeded from a rolling
hash of the PUBLIC event stream, so any observer replays it.  `rtie=2` draws from
a per-seat stream keyed off that seat's own reset seed, which no observer and no
teammate can replay — and v7side's S4 confirms the distinction mechanically:
rtie=1 gives 400/400 identical transcripts under an independent per-seat stream,
rtie=2 gives 0/400.

  Price of replacing the public rule with the private draw, paired head to head:
  -0.31 [-1.20, +0.59] over 24,000 games, replicated in sign on both banks, and
  BOTH banks slightly favour the RANDOM version.

So the Price of Uncorrelation at this decision point is zero to within about
+-0.9 points.  It is also worth nothing, because phase 1 left no readability
handicap for it to buy.  RECOMMENDATION: keep `rtie=1` — not because randomising
is expensive but because determinism is free and is what makes S4's strongest
form (400/400 transcript identity) available.  The limb is closed.

8. THE F-CHEAP S6 ANOMALY — chased down; it is not what it looked like
=============================================================================
The brief inherited one un-certified panel member: F-cheap fails S6 on bank
7030002 with 1 irreproducible decision in 264,051, clean on 7030001.

  --tests=s6,          threads=2, seed 7030002:  0 / 264,075  (run twice, same)
  --tests=s6,          threads=4, seed 7030002:  0 / 264,075
  --tests=s6,          threads=1, seed 7030002:  1 / 264,061
  --tests=s6,          threads=2, seed 7030001:  0 / 262,920  (clean control)
  --tests=s3,s4,s5,s6, threads=2, seed 7030002:  1 / 264,051  (reproduces K0)
  --tests=s4,s6,       threads=2, seed 7030002:  1 / 264,051  (S4 alone suffices)

Two facts fall out.  First, the mismatch is not a property of a decision: it
appears and disappears with the thread count and with whether S4's second
game-pass runs.  Second, and this is the real finding, the DECISION-NODE COUNT
itself takes four different values — 264,037 / 264,051 / 264,061 / 264,075 —
depending on those same two knobs.  The harness's play is therefore a function of
how deals are grouped onto reused agent objects; the search carries state across
deals; and S6 is partly measuring the harness it runs in.

THE IMPORTANT NEGATIVE: this does NOT impugn any strength number in the corpus.
`match` results are bit-stable across thread counts:
  v06 vs v05     threads 1/2/3:  winRateA 0.535000, ev/g 95.535000,
                                 askAccA 0.547877, declAccA 0.985355 — all equal
  F-cheap vs v06 threads 1/2/3:  winRateA 0.508750, ev/g 95.147500,
                                 askAccA 0.538021, declAccA 0.983380 — all equal

A CANDIDATE MECHANISM, FOUND AND THEN KILLED.  `RolloutEngine::playOut` seats the
six blueprint agents by assigning `sim.agents[p] = ag[p].get()` and never resets
them; `Game::emit` calls `agents[p]->observe(e)` (game.hpp:260); and
`RolloutEngine::seatAgents()` — the function written to do exactly that reset —
has NO CALLER ANYWHERE IN THE TREE.  So the blueprints accumulate the events of
every rollout of every decision of every deal.  That is real dead code and a real
hygiene gap, and it is NOT the cause:
  * with `rreset=1` (blueprints re-seated per rollout) S6 on 7030002 gives
    2/264,037 at threads=1 and 1/264,051 at threads=2 — no better, and at
    threads=2 bit-for-bit the same failure; and
  * `rreset=1` changes match play not at all: F-cheap+rreset vs v06 on 7030001,
    800 games, gives winRateA 0.508750, ev/g 95.147500, askAccA 0.538021 —
    identical to six decimals to F-cheap without it.
The switch ships default-off with the identity control intact.  Recorded as a
negative, and as a lead for whoever owns the search: the rollout blueprints'
accumulated observations have zero measurable effect on the search's output,
which says something about how little those blueprints are actually using.
The larger paired strength cell for `rreset=1` was killed by the harness lifetime
and did not complete.
