4. THE MECHANISM — the proxy moved and the outcome did not
===============================================================================
Per-decision rates on the EVALUATION banks, as deltas from v06-as-arm-A
(reference from the 48-block design-effect probe: askAcc 54.080%, declAcc
97.806%, allocErrShare 1.939%):

  objective   d askAcc   d declAcc   d allocErrShare   win-rate edge
  win         +0.80 pp    +0.23 pp      -0.15 pp          +0.40
  selfdecl    -2.33 pp    +0.39 pp      -0.17 pp          -2.26
  selfask     +2.10 pp    -0.34 pp      +0.18 pp          -1.59
  selfalloc   -0.72 pp    -0.07 pp      -0.28 pp          -1.20

Every per-decision fit moved its OWN proxy in the intended direction, on both
evaluation banks, and lost win rate.  Two further readings:

 (a) The ledger's §0.2 conversion FAILS out of sample.  +0.385 pp of declaration
     accuracy should be worth +0.46 win-rate points; it delivered -2.26.  The
     conversion was fitted on v0.6-vs-v0.5, where declaration accuracy was
     essentially the only thing that moved.  A fitter free to move 55
     coordinates holds nothing else fixed, so the conversion is a
     ceteris-paribus law and the ledger's expected-gain ranking uses it as a
     sufficient statistic.
 (b) The two per-decision channels TRADE AGAINST EACH OTHER.  selfdecl bought
     +0.39 pp of declaration accuracy and paid -2.33 pp of ask accuracy; selfask
     bought +2.10 pp of ask accuracy and paid -0.34 pp of declaration accuracy.
     Neither trade is favourable.  This is the v0.5->v0.6 fact (v0.6 LOST 2.3 pp
     of ask hit rate and GAINED win rate) reproduced with the sign reversed, at
     almost the same magnitude.

8. THE F-CHEAP S6 ARTIFACT — RESOLVED, and it is not a channel
===============================================================================
The brief flags one irreproducible F-cheap ask on bank 7030002 (1 in 264,051)
and asks which it is.  Same spec, same bank, S6 only:

  threads=2, run 1:   0 / 264,075 irreproducible   PASS
  threads=2, run 2:   0 / 264,075 irreproducible   PASS   (identical)
  threads=1, run 1:   1 / 264,061 irreproducible   FAIL
  threads=1, run 2:   1 / 264,061 irreproducible   FAIL   (identical)
  threads=2, bank 7030001 control: 0 / 262,920     PASS

It is NOT a race: a race is non-deterministic AT 2 threads and clean at 1; this
is exactly reproducible WITHIN each thread count and differs BETWEEN them.  The
decision COUNT itself moves with execution context — 264,051 (brief, all four
tests) / 264,061 (1 thread) / 264,075 (2 threads) for one spec on one bank.
=> The F-cheap search's transcript is a deterministic function of (deal, thread
   count), not of the deal alone.  Its determinization consumes from a stream
   whose consumption depends on execution context.  The S6 "failure" is a
   symptom of that, not of an illegal channel — but it means no search number in
   the corpus is bit-reproducible across thread settings, and v7side's S6
   verdict for a searching configuration is context-dependent.  That is worth a
   ledger entry and it is not what phase 2 assumed.

10. WHAT WAS NOT DONE
===============================================================================
* No fit against a PANEL containing F-cheap.  At 2 threads a 21,600-game fit
  against the search is ~16 minutes of pure search time per fit; the budget went
  to replicating the core comparison at a second seed instead.  Fitted against
  v06, EVALUATED against F-fast and F-cheap, as the brief permits.
* No r12 attribution.  It needs three new sub-product coordinates and a dose
  sweep on each; the matched-budget comparison and its replication consumed the
  budget.  The design-effect table in §1 is the instrument it would use, and it
  says a declaration-side attribution cell costs ~300 games rather than ~9,600.
* selfask and selfalloc were fitted at one seed only.
* No budget curve.  Every fit here is at phase 1's small rung (21,600 games).
  Whether the per-decision objective wins at 4x the budget is untested, and it
  is the one honest way the kill in §3 could be wrong.
