## Throughput -- the cost claim, CHECKED rather than repeated

`gamesPerSec` is WHOLE-MATCH throughput: it carries the opponent's decision cost on both
sides of the ratio, so INCUMBENT_gps / ARM_gps = (t_arm + t_opp)/(t_inc + t_opp), which is
strictly BELOW t_arm/t_inc.  Every figure below is therefore a LOWER BOUND on the cost
multiple, and the bound is tightest against the cheapest opponents.

arm         vs `random` (tightest) far archetypes (median) whole panel (median)  
FROZEN      4.52x                  4.68x                  2.76x                 
F-cheap     4.10x                  4.37x                  2.66x                 
composite   4.20x                  4.52x                  2.72x                 

The tightest bound is the one taken against the cheapest opponents, where the opponent
contributes least to both sides of the ratio.  The protocol records the configuration as
running at "roughly 3.2x the blueprint" and asks phase 5 to CHECK rather than repeat it.
Measured here: FROZEN costs AT LEAST 4.52x the blueprint against `random`, and at least
the far-archetype median above.  Both lower bounds EXCEED 3.2x, so the recorded cost
claim understates the true multiple rather than overstating it.  The whole-panel median
is lower only because the expensive panel members put a large common term on both sides
of the ratio; it is the most diluted of the three and the least informative.

## Panel duplicates -- a free determinism check, and what it caught

Three sealed adversaries are policies the scripted archetypes and the frontier already
carry (R-v04 is v04, S-archetype-0 is feint, S-reference-0 is v06).  The protocol fixes the
panel by NAME, so those cells are run anyway.  Each duplicated pair is the SAME command
run twice -- same arm, same opponent spec, same bank, same deals, same 13 threads -- so
the two cells must agree to the digit.

24 duplicated (arm, policy, bank) cells; 1 disagree.
  DISAGREEMENT  arm=F-cheap  opponent=feint  bank=7090002
    feint 0.543250  SEALED:S-archetype-0 0.543333
    difference 0.000083 = 1 game(s) in 12000 = 0.0083 points

  This is the cross-deal agent residue of PREREGISTRATION 5.3, surfacing in the SCORED
  mode for the first time in this corpus.  `runMatch` schedules deals by work-stealing
  over a shared atomic counter (arena.hpp), so the deal-to-thread assignment is not
  fixed run to run even at a fixed thread count; agents are built once per thread and
  reused across deals, and some per-agent state survives reset() and is reachable only
  under the search.  A searching arm can therefore play one deal differently between
  two invocations of the identical command.  The protocol quantifies the defect as
  "one deal in 800 of play" and states that phase 5 evaluates the frozen configuration
  AS FROZEN, with the defect present.

  The B0 reproducibility check did not catch this: it ran a 400-deal cell five times
  and got bit-identical results.  The panel duplicates are 1,500- and 6,000-deal cells,
  and at that size the residue bites.  The B0 check was therefore too small to be
  conclusive, and this is the correction to it.

  Magnitude, against the numbers it could move: the largest disagreement above is
  0.0083 points, against a per-cell half-width of 0.63 points -- about 76x smaller.
  No verdict in this document turns on a quantity that small, and every arm carries
  the same defect, but it is a real property of the instrument and it is recorded.
