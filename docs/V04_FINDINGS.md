# FishLab v0.4 findings

Scope: six players, 54 cards, nine half-suits, wrong declarations awarded to the
opposing team, declarations legal at any moment. All figures below come from
seed banks disjoint from those used for fitting; the configuration was frozen
before they were run. Regenerate everything with `engine/experiments.sh`.

The numeric tables are generated by `engine/build_tables.py` into
`docs/V04_RESULTS.md`; this document describes the design and what it
establishes. The full study is `paper/fishbot_v04.tex`.

## Three defects the audit found, and what they were worth

An independent audit of the paper against the code and the raw artifacts found
three implementation defects that had survived earlier testing. All are fixed;
all are named here because two of them invalidate the provenance of earlier
numbers.

1. **The flat parameter vector was parsed with a hard-coded offset.** When the
   ask-feature count grew from 18 to 20, two ask weights were aliased onto the
   first two decision knobs, so the vector the optimiser scored was not the
   vector `freeze_config.py` baked in. There is now a round-trip assertion:
   `v04:allparams=<frozen vector>` must reproduce the shipped defaults exactly.
2. **The six-rotation duplicate block never exchanged hand-triples.** The team
   label and the hand shift were both driven by the rotation index, so one
   policy held the even-indexed deal in all six rotations. Fixed by varying them
   independently: self-play now returns exactly three wins per six rotations on
   every deal, and the cluster interval is narrower than the Wilson interval
   rather than wider.
3. **The fitted policy prior never reached the ask posterior** — only the
   declaration estimator. Connecting it was worth about six win-rate points on
   its own.

Reported intervals are now cluster bootstraps over deals, as the methodology
always claimed; declaration races are arbitrated by seat rather than by
comparing private confidences.

## What the study establishes

1. The belief engine is **sound**: across the verification sweep no agent ever
   excluded the true holder of a card, no derived capacity disagreed with the
   truth, and no ask-legality certificate was violated by the actual deal.
2. The exact posterior is **validated three ways**: against an independent
   capacity dynamic program (machine precision), against exact rejection
   sampling (Monte Carlo error), and against brute force on small instances.
3. **Ask-legality certificates dominate** the ablations, as the v0.3 study's
   ask-history result predicted, but as a logical certificate rather than a
   fitted weight.
4. **Declaration timing is a first-order decision**, which follows from the
   locked-half-suit theorem, and modelling it as optimal stopping is worth
   measurable win rate.
5. The agent's **own probability forecasts are calibrated**, which is what makes
   the stopping rule a decision rule rather than a heuristic.
6. **Termination is a property of the policy, not of the rules.** Because a
   half-suit frozen by the locked-half-suit theorem also admits no further
   information, two correctly patient policies deadlock permanently; a fitted
   configuration failed to terminate in 21% of self-play deals at any action cap
   tried. A graduated in-policy forcing rule removes it entirely. See
   `research/v04/results/E11-termination.md`.
7. **The agent is far harder to counter than its predecessor.** An adversary
   fitted specifically against FishBot v0.4, in the same policy class and with
   the same optimiser, fails to reach 50% against it; the identical procedure
   beats FishBot v0.3 comfortably. This is a lower bound on exploitability, not
   a bound.
